# Educational YouTube channels for children

Research date: 2026-08-17

## Recommendation

Start with the first six channels below as Approved Content. They offer a useful mix of science, engineering, nature/geography, mathematics, art, and music without relying on gaming entertainment. Add TED-Ed and NASA STEM selectively for an older or especially curious Child.

The age bands below are practical editorial estimates, not platform ratings. Even reputable channels can change, carry sponsorships, or publish an occasional video that is a poor fit for a particular Child. An Admin should preview recent uploads and prefer a specific playlist when approving an entire channel would be too broad.

## Suggested first set by age

- Ages 3–6: Numberblocks, with selected SciShow Kids episodes.
- Ages 6–9: SciShow Kids, National Geographic Kids, Tate Kids, and Classics for Kids.
- Ages 9–12: add Crash Course Kids and Design Squad Global; use selected NASA STEM and TED-Ed videos.

## Additional recommendations for age 9

These are additions to the five channels already selected for Julian: Crash Course Kids, Design Squad Global, National Geographic Kids, SciShow Kids, and Art for Kids Hub. For a nine-year-old, the strongest next additions are **Math Antics**, **Scratch Team**, and **Storyline Online**. They broaden the library into mathematics, creative coding, and reading without simply adding more general science videos.

The age guidance remains an editorial estimate rather than an official rating. Rows marked “selected” should be approved as individual videos or a narrow playlist, not as an unrestricted whole-channel recommendation.

**Math Antics duration check (2026-08-17):** These are not generally long videos for a nine-year-old. A metadata check of all 104 uploads on the [official YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@mathantics/videos) found that the 92 regular lessons have a median duration of 10:13 (average 9:51), while the 12 short “Extras”/channel-update uploads have a median of 4:35. The whole catalog ranges from 1:43 to 14:31, with no video at or above 15 minutes and no long compilation outlier; representative recent full lessons, [What Are Fractions?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I1u3pM9g9o8) and [Fractions on the Number Line](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pWJzqTYS8no), run 12:05 and 12:47. The [official Math Antics site](https://www.mathantics.com/) identifies these as free video lessons and organizes them by math topic. Method: durations were read from YouTube’s first-party channel/video metadata, then grouped by titles marked “Extras” or “Channel Update” versus regular lessons.

### Suggested order

1. Add **Math Antics** first for a genuinely new subject area.
2. Add **Scratch Team** if Julian is willing to create projects rather than only watch.
3. Add **Storyline Online** if English listening and reading are priorities.
4. Choose **Tate Kids** or **Classics for Kids** according to his interest in visual art or music.
5. Treat **Deep Look**, **TED-Ed**, and **NASA STEM** as sources for parent-curated playlists rather than blanket channel approvals.

There is no equally strong, uniformly child-safe general-history channel in this shortlist. Selected TED-Ed lessons are the better option: they allow an Admin to choose the period and emotional intensity rather than inheriting a mixed catalog simply to fill a subject category.

## Beast Academy and TED-Ed riddles for Julian

### Beast Academy

The official YouTube channel is **Beast Academy Math**, handle **[@beastacademymath6671](https://www.youtube.com/@beastacademymath6671)**, stable channel ID **`UC7L0m6dn6UsVTcsVwDbjT8Q`** ([stable channel URL](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC7L0m6dn6UsVTcsVwDbjT8Q)). Its first-party channel description identifies Beast Academy as a comprehensive comic-based elementary mathematics curriculum for ages 5–13, designed by leaders in advanced mathematics education, and points viewers to Beast Academy Playground. The [official Beast Academy About page](https://beastacademy.com/about) presents the program as an advanced mathematics program for elementary students and places it inside the Art of Problem Solving product family; the [official AoPS site](https://artofproblemsolving.com/discover) likewise links Beast Academy as one of its offerings.

**Content decision for a mathematically strong nine-year-old:** the publisher and subject are appropriate, but approving the whole channel is not especially useful as a video library. A first-party YouTube metadata check on 2026-08-17 found only 44 uploads. Thirty-seven are at most three minutes, mostly instructions for offline Playground games aimed broadly at ages 3/4/5+, and ZTube intentionally excludes videos of three minutes or less. Six of the seven longer uploads are parent, teacher, or administrator product walkthroughs; the remaining child-facing problem video, [The Elevator Problem](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g6mr6HWtShw), is only 2:08 and is therefore also excluded by ZTube. Whole-channel approval is safe in subject matter but will produce little or no child-facing playable content under the current duration rule. Beast Academy's books, online curriculum, and Playground activities are much deeper than this small YouTube catalog.

**Duration detail:** Across all 44 uploads, durations range from 0:48 to 7:21, with a median of 1:52 and an average of 2:20. The 37 uploads at or under three minutes range from 0:48 to 2:47, with a median of 1:48 and an average of 1:44: one is under one minute, 26 are from 1:00 through 1:59, and 10 are from 2:00 through 2:59; none is exactly 3:00. Of those 37 short uploads, 35 demonstrate a Playground game or activity, one introduces Playground, and one is *The Elevator Problem*. These figures come from the duration and title metadata on the [official channel's Videos tab](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC7L0m6dn6UsVTcsVwDbjT8Q/videos).

### TED-Ed riddles

TED-Ed has one clearly relevant official YouTube playlist: **[Can You Solve This Riddle?](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLJicmE8fK0EiFRt1Hm5a_7SJFaikIFW30)**, playlist ID **`PLJicmE8fK0EiFRt1Hm5a_7SJFaikIFW30`**, published by the official [TED-Ed channel](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCsooa4yRKGN_zEE8iknghZA). Its first-party description calls it a brain-busting riddle series testing concentration, cunning, and puzzle-solving ability. On 2026-08-17 it contained 83 videos; durations run from 3:25 to 6:24, with a median of 5:06 and an average of about 5:01.

**Approval decision:** this is the exact official riddle playlist, but a curated subset is safer for age nine than approving all 83 entries. The playlist mixes neutral mathematics and logic with theatrical premises involving prisoners and jail, viruses, pirates, death, Ragnarok, werewolves and vampires, Hades, the Trojan War, killer robots, evil wizards, demons, assassins, crime, curses, and Pandora's box. These are puzzle narratives rather than evidence that the lessons are graphic, but the official titles alone show that the full playlist is not uniformly gentle or age-targeted.

## Approval note

YouTube’s recommendations, comments, advertisements, and linked videos are separate from the educational merit of the publisher. ZTube’s Approved Content boundary should remain the control: preview the channel’s current uploads, use a playlist or individual video when the catalog is mixed, and do not treat “Made for Kids” as an educational-quality rating.
